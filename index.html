01Strong

Wait for the rebound

Our ask: Before closing Sunrise, set a public enrollment threshold and check it again in fall 2028, after the small class has moved up.

  • Sunrise's kindergarten classes run 30–33 students. The 2024 class had 18. That class is now moving through the grades and leaves after 2029.S11
  • The district projects Sunrise's resident students bottoming out at 189 in 2028 and rising to 206 by 2030 and 210 by 2034.S3, S11
  • Before any Moorlands students are moved, the district projects Arrowhead (204) below Sunrise (206) in 2030.S3

Trade-off: Waiting means the district doesn't collect closure savings in the meantime. Scenario 2 would save about $2.2M a year.

02Strong

If consolidating, choose Scenario 3

Our ask: Weigh the scenarios on full cost and disruption, not on which building has the fewest students today.

  • Scenario 3 keeps Sunrise and saves more than Scenario 2 in both of the district's cases: $2.34M vs. $2.22M (Case A) and $2.18M vs. $2.17M (Case B).S1
  • 14 walkers become bus riders, vs. 158 under Scenario 2. The longest ride is 49 minutes, vs. 51.S2
  • It avoids $17.4M in repairs scheduled for 2028–29, after closures could take effect, vs. $13.0M for Scenario 2. calcS5

Trade-offs: Scenario 3 changes schools for 44 more students districtwide (945 vs. 901) and adds two east-side bus routes. It also closes Hollywood Hill, whose families deserve the same care we're asking for.

03Strong

Count the full cost

Our ask: For each scenario, publish one-time closure costs, the real added cost of busing, sewage hauling at receiving schools, upkeep of closed buildings, and expected enrollment loss.

  • The posted savings count fixed building costs and added office staff at receiving schools. They leave out one-time, transportation and building-upkeep costs.S1, S12
  • At the district's average cost per rider, Scenario 2's 158 new bus riders cost about $254,000 a year. That's 11% of its savings. calcS2, S8
  • Under Scenario 2, most Sunrise students would go to Cottage Lake, which trucks its sewage out. The hauling cost isn't published.S6

04Moderate

Fill Sunrise's empty seats

Our ask: Add an option to the study that brings Sunrise above 300 students, through boundary changes or by placing a program there.

  • Sunrise has 245 students and 429 planned seats.S1, S4
  • Fixed cost per student is about $5,100 today. At 330 students it would be about $3,800. calcS1
  • In Scenario 3 the district already lists two Hollywood Hill pockets that border Sunrise (HH0 and HH2, 83 students).S7
  • Of the six schools we checked, Sunrise is the only one confirmed on public sewer, and it's inside the growth area, so it's the easiest site to add students to.S6

Trade-off: Students who move to Sunrise leave somewhere else. Moving the Hollywood Hill pockets alone would drop Hollywood Hill to about 274 students. A program or choice option avoids that.

05Needs data

Retire portables first

Our ask: Have EDAC compare the cost of removing portable classrooms with closing a building, since both reduce extra seats.

  • The district has about 13,000 elementary design seats and about 9,300 students projected for 2030, including students from outside the district. calcS3
  • There are 61 portable classrooms across ten elementary schools, about 1,370 seats. That's close to the 1,688 seats Scenario 1 removes by closing three schools. calcS3

What's missing: The annual cost of keeping a portable. We're asking the district for it.
