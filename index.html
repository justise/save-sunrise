Where things stand

The district is weighing three closure scenarios

Northshore School District says east-side elementary enrollment has fallen and is uneven across schools. On October 26 it presented three scenarios. Sunrise closes in two of them. Nothing is final, and there's still time to be heard.

What happens next

    The facts

    What the district's own data show about Sunrise

    Enrollment is leveling off and then rising

    The number of children living in the Sunrise boundary fell from 247 to 197 between 2023 and 2026. The district's own projection has it flattening at about 190 and then climbing to 206 by 2030. That's about 9% growth from 2028 to 2030, the fastest of any school in these scenarios.S3

    Families also choose Sunrise: 245 students attend, about 48 more than live in the boundary.

    Resident students are children who live in an attendance area. That's different from enrollment, which counts who attends.

    Show as table

    Sunrise is a site the district can grow on

    Sunrise sits inside the Urban Growth Area on 11 acres with public sewer. Hollywood Hill, Cottage Lake and East Ridge are outside the growth area. Cottage Lake, East Ridge and Wellington truck their sewage out.S6

    A closed school is hard to get back. If enrollment returns, Sunrise is the east-side building best placed to take more students.

    Most of the "savings" would be people, not the building

    The district counts $1.25 million a year in fixed costs for Sunrise after its 5% adjustment. Only about $287,000 of that is the building itself: utilities, facility manager and custodian.S1

    The rest pays the principal, counselor, nurse, librarian and office staff who serve Sunrise kids every day.

    A better path

    Alternatives we're asking the board to consider

    We don't think the board should close Sunrise. If it decides closures are needed, the district's own figures don't favor closing Sunrise.

    The district's own scenario that keeps Sunrise saves more

    Scenario 3 keeps Sunrise open. By the district's estimate it saves $2.34M a year (Case A), more than Scenario 2, which closes Sunrise ($2.22M). It saves more in Case B too. It turns 14 walkers into bus riders, compared with 158 under Scenario 2.S1, S2

    The district's stated objection is that Sunrise "would still be the lowest enrolled school in the district." Scenario 3 sends about 70 Hollywood Hill students to Sunrise. That fills seats the district already pays to maintain.

    1

    Fill Sunrise instead of emptying it

    Sunrise has 429 planned seats and 245 students, which leaves about 184 open seats.calc Nearby Hollywood Hill and Wellington still count on 102 portable seats between them.S4

    • Adjust boundaries so nearby neighborhoods attend Sunrise
    • Move students out of portables and into permanent classrooms
    2

    Put a program at Sunrise

    Sunrise is inside the growth area on public sewer, so it's the easiest east-side site to add students to.

    • Place a preschool, regional or specialty program at Sunrise
    • Use open rooms for before- and after-school care that working families need
    3

    Wait for the rebound

    The district projects Sunrise resident students to bottom out in 2028 and rise to 206 by 2030.S3 Closure is permanent; projections can be checked.

    • Revisit enrollment in 2–3 years before any permanent closure
    • Set a clear, public enrollment threshold now
    4

    Publish the full cost before voting

    The posted savings leave out costs that could shrink the gap between scenarios.

    • One-time closure and moving costs
    • Added busing and longer rides
    • Extra sewage hauling at receiving schools
    • State funding lost if families leave the district
    • Upkeep of a closed building
